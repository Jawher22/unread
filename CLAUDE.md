# Unread

macOS menu-bar app that reads on-screen text aloud with an on-device natural voice. Point at a paragraph, hold ⌃, listen. Free, open source, personal tool first.

**Read [`docs/design.md`](docs/design.md) before making product or architecture decisions.** It holds the research, the interaction design and its reasoning, technical findings, measurements and the prioritized next steps.

## Non-negotiables

- **Effortless is the product.** The user never selects text. Every feature should reduce effort, not add a step.
- **Hotkey is ⌃ Control.** The author runs Hex (voice dictation) on ⌥ Option, so never use Option.
- **Local-first.** On-device TTS by default. Cloud voices could be an optional bring-your-own-key extra, never the default.
- **Fast feel, the Hex playbook:** models stay warm, work starts on key-down, synthesis runs ahead of playback. Measure latency; don't guess.
- **One voice: the narrator** (Qwen3-TTS 1.7B, a designed preset per language). Kokoro, the first voice and later the fallback, was dropped on 2026-11-05, leaving one pipeline.
- **Languages:** English first, French second, others later.

## Build, run, test

```bash
scripts/build.sh                      # release build; Swift 6.2 toolchain; macOS 15+, Apple Silicon. Not a bare `swift build` (see below)
./.build/release/Unread               # menu-bar app; needs Accessibility permission
./.build/release/Unread --extract     # print sentences + bounds Unread would read from the front window
./.build/release/Unread --at x,y [--pid N] [--wait S] [--after S]  # what a hold at this point picks, what each of its lookups costs (ms, AX calls), and the first sentences from it
./.build/release/Unread --simulate x,y [seconds] [--pid N [--pointed | --from-text "…"]] [--speed X] [--record out.wav] [--voice id] [--primed [S]] [--speed-change-at T] [--jump-at T] [--idle-before S [--evict] [--no-wake]]  # muted reading from a point (the highlight runs invisibly; --show-highlight draws it); --pid reads that app from the top, --pointed from where a hold at x,y would start in it, --from-text from the first sentence holding the text; --voice reads English in that preset (default the London storyteller); --record writes what the player outputs, pauses included, on the player's timeline; --primed starts from a hold released S s (0.4) after speculation began; --jump-at holds to jump back to the start; --idle-before leaves the narrator idle first (--evict: its mapped pages dropped), then ⌃ wakes it 0.3 s before the speculation
./.build/release/Unread --stretch-check  # silent: the narrator's time stretch at every menu speed (scripts/test.sh runs it)
./.build/release/Unread --stats       # p50/p95 latency from ~/Library/Logs/Unread/latency.jsonl
./.build/release/Unread --hear ["sentence" …] [--voice id] [--raw]  # silent: what the narrator says for each sentence (as reading gives it; --raw: as written), transcribed by the aligner's model
./.build/release/Unread --speakable ["sentence" …]  # what the narrator is given for each sentence (#18), where it differs; reads stdin lines without arguments (pipe a fixture's expected.txt)
./.build/release/Unread --speakable-check  # silent: the #18 rules against their table (scripts/test.sh runs it)
./.build/release/Unread --pdf-check  # silent: how Chromium's PDF viewer is read (#85), from tables of blocks: furniture, footnotes, figures, columns and pages (scripts/test.sh runs it)
./.build/release/Unread --align ["sentence" …]  # silent: forced alignment (#58) of the narrator's words, against the estimate and partway, its cost, and whether it slows the narrator
./.build/release/Unread --align --check out.wav session.log [--verbose]  # a reading's scheduled word starts (UNREAD_DEBUG_OVERLAY=1 --simulate … --record out.wav) against an alignment of what it played, and how many words each "Mark ahead of voice" setting shows noticeably late or early
./.build/release/Unread --narrate ["sentence" …] [--voice id] [--out f.wav] [--runs N] [--cancel-test] [--long-test] [--switch-test [--cold]]  # silent: the narrator voice: load, first audio, speed, stalls, pitch, GPU time; --voice reads its language in that preset; --switch-test switches through every preset (--cold: clip encoded afresh)
./.build/release/Unread --narrate --idle 0,30,… [--wake none,prefetch,warm,both] [--lead 0.3] [--evict] [--churn f,…]  # silent: a short sentence after each idle, with what ⌃ going down does first: stages, page-ins, how much of the mapped model files was in memory
./.build/release/Unread --eval PASSAGES --out DIR [--paragraph] [--temperature T] [--top-k K] [--repetition R] [--voice id] [--seed-salt S] [--speed X [--stretch Y] [--squeeze P]] [--reference-stretch R]  # silent: each passage read as a reading says it (sayable text, carried paragraphs, Pacing's pauses), one WAV each; --paragraph gives the narrator whole paragraphs (#79); --speed plays it at X the way reading does (time stretch, pauses ÷ X); --reference-stretch clones from the clip compressed R×. Driven by scripts/eval.sh
./.build/release/Unread --pace [--reference-stretch 1.15,1.25] [--out DIR] [--speeds 1.2,1.3]  # silent: each voice's natural pace (voiced c/s), pitch, and the narrator cloned from a time-compressed clip; --out writes one passage read by each
./.build/release/Unread --settings-snapshot out.png [seconds] [--tab voices|highlight] [--dark]  # render the Settings window off-screen (no screen recording needed)
./.build/release/Unread --highlight-frames out.png|out.gif [from] [to] [--style mark=tail,motion=flow] [--zoom x,y,w,h]  # the demo frame by frame, to review motion
```

```bash
scripts/bundle.sh [--install]         # signed Unread.app in build/ (--install: copy to ~/Applications and restart it); logs: ~/Library/Logs/Unread/unread.log
```

- The bundle is signed with the first "Apple Development" identity so the Accessibility grant survives rebuilds. Dev modes work from the bundle too: `build/Unread.app/Contents/MacOS/Unread --extract`.

- The first clean build compiles FluidAudio (2–15 min depending on cache). After that, rebuilds take seconds.
- Never copy `.build` between folders: Swift's precompiled headers bake in absolute paths and the build fails.
- The narrator's model set (2.88 GB) is fetched by `scripts/fetch-narrator.sh` (pinned revision, sha256 per file, resumable; `--from DIR` copies matching files from a local copy) into `~/Library/Application Support/Unread/models/Qwen3-TTS-1.7B-Base-ANE`. It isn't bundled. Without it, the app has no voice: the menu says to run the script, and ⌃ beeps. Until it has loaded (a few seconds after launch), ⌃ beeps too.
- The aligner's model (Parakeet CTC 110M, ~100 MB) is downloaded by FluidAudio on first use into `~/.cache/fluidaudio`.
- **Build with `scripts/build.sh`, never a bare `swift build -c release`.** SwiftPM 6.2 emits FluidAudio's trait defines in random Set order, so a bare build can recompile FluidAudio (~60 s) with nothing changed, and costs the next scripted build the same. The script sets `SWIFT_DETERMINISTIC_HASHING=1`.
- `scripts/test.sh` builds, then checks every fixture page in both web engines (WebKit and Electron windows that are invisible and click-through), and `.txt`/`.rtf` pages in a native text view, against its `<page>.expected.txt`, all in parallel: ~20 s, then ~10 s scoring the local corpus if one is saved; ~5 s with `--quick` (skips `rerender.html`, which pauses 1.6 s per sentence). Run it before and after any change to `Document.swift`. One page with full output: `scripts/extract-fixture.sh fixtures/pages/<page>.html`. See `fixtures/README.md`.
- `scripts/window-check.sh [page]` checks that the reading highlight belongs to its window (~15 s once the narrator is compiled): a hidden WebKit host (`--levers`) moves, resizes, covers and raises its own window while a muted `--simulate` reads it, and `scripts/window-check.swift` reports from the window server the stacking order after each step and the panel's lag behind the glide (`WINDOW_CHECK_VERBOSE=1`: every position). Logs in `build/window-check/`. Scrolling or switching Spaces during a run shows up in it.
- `scripts/make-voices.sh` makes the narrator's voice presets (#60) from `scripts/voices/voices.json` (descriptions, reference sentences, preview text): VoiceDesign says each reference sentence several times, Whisper checks every take against its transcript, the best is installed in `Resources/Voices`, the app's own narrator reads the preview, Whisper checks that too, and it's stored as AAC. Each step skips what's made, so a new voice is one entry and a rerun. Its venv (`build/voices/.venv`) is made from the pinned `scripts/voices/requirements.txt` with uv. VoiceDesign and Whisper use the GPU (~7 GB), at utility priority, one model at a time. `scripts/make-voices.sh report` lists every candidate and why it was rejected; `audition DIR` copies the previews out with readable names.
- `scripts/eval.sh` judges the narrator's reading quality (#75, #80) on 16 fixed passages (`fixtures/eval.txt`: corpus excerpts, plus originals in `fixtures/eval-original/`): `passages` assembles them, `render NAME [flags]` reads them with `--eval`, `elevenlabs` makes the ElevenLabs v4 references once (`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`), `score NAME…` gives Whisper word errors, pitch spread and pauses, `ab A B` serves a blind A/B page, `rate NAME` scores a run 1–5 against the references. A change ships if it's preferred in at least 60% of the blind pairs with no more word errors. Everything under `fixtures/eval/` is local.
- `scripts/score-corpus.sh` scores real pages (Anthropic, OpenAI, Cloudflare, Simon Willison, Dan Luu, Wikipedia) in both engines: sentence precision, recall and bounds per page, against a recorded baseline. The snapshots hold third-party text, so they're local only (`fixtures/corpus/`, gitignored); only the manifest `fixtures/corpus.txt` is committed, and `scripts/save-corpus.sh` saves them. `scripts/test.sh` prints the table; a fresh clone has none. Run it before and after an extraction change.
- `--extract` and `--narrate` are the safe ways to verify changes without hijacking the user's keyboard or playing audio. Ask before launching the full app or playing sound on the user's machine.
- **Testing never draws on the user's screen.** Fixture hosts run with `--hidden` (transparent, click-through), and `--simulate` keeps its highlight invisible; pass `--show-highlight` only when the user asks to see it. Open documents in real apps only in the background (`open -g`), never activate them, and post keys only to their pid.

## Hard-won constraints (details in docs/design.md)

- **Chromium/Electron AX is a subset of WebKit's.** These fail in Chromium:
  - `AXTextMarkerForPosition`
  - `AXStartTextMarkerForBounds`
  - `AXNextSentenceEndTextMarkerForTextMarker`

  These work in both engines:
  - element hit-testing + `AXTextMarkerRangeForUIElement`
  - paragraph ranges and word/char stepping
  - `AXStringForTextMarkerRange` and `AXBoundsForTextMarkerRange`

  `WebDocument` deliberately uses only this common subset. Keep it that way.
- Chromium reports each **wrapped line** as a paragraph and joins blocks with **no separator**. That's why sentences are cut from a ~600-character window and block breaks are detected via a shared ancestor.
- Chromium sometimes returns viewport-sized bounds. Treat those as "no bounds".
- **Markers must be anchored to a text leaf (`AXStaticText`).** A marker taken from a container's range (the web area, a hit-tested message block) keeps that anchor through every navigation call, and Chromium then answers every bounds query with the container's box. Hit-test results are resolved down to the leaf under the point (`WebDocument.textLeaf`).
- **Chromium measures a range precisely only if both ends are inside a leaf.** Sentence bounds are therefore the union of per-word boxes, dropping words that come back taller than the line pitch or wider than a word can be (`WordExtent`), and re-measuring those, and words that come back with no box, from a few characters in; a word at a line's start takes its left edge from its leaf's frame (an average character's width missed "much" by 7 px). A word still without a box keeps its place in the letter match (`WordBoxes.assign`), or its letters are taken from the words after it ("the" once got a 232 px box over three words). Chromium's word box starts at the space before the word (~4 px); it's cut at that space's right edge (`withoutLeadingSpace`).
- **Chromium's hit test is unreliable** (in T3 Code it can return a 1 px button that doesn't contain the point). Use a hit only if it contains the point and holds text; otherwise descend from the web area by frame. Never anchor a marker to a container.
- **Chromium can switch its tree off again** (auto-disable), leaving the focused "window" as an `AXApplication` stub. `AXManualAccessibility` always reads back false, so `AX.enableTree` judges by tree health: on first contact it sets the flag only if no `AXWebArea` exists yet (setting it restarts the build), and later re-enables a stub tree at most every 10 s.
- **Highlights are per line** (`ReadableDocument.lines(of:)`), drawn as one shape; a single union box covers the neighbouring sentences.
- **Word and line boxes sit on the glyphs (`GlyphBand`).** WebKit, and Books through its iOS bridge, measure a range as the union of its two end carets: on a block's first line that's the glyphs, on every later line it reaches up to the line above's text bottom, leading included (20 px Georgia: 22 px, then 40 at `line-height: 2` and 60 at 3; Books 27, then 32). The bottom is the text's either way. Every box keeps its bottom and is cut to the band (never grown), and the boxes on a line share it. The band: in WebKit, the font's ascent + descent from the attributed string's `AXFont` (an uninstalled font is 1.15 em), or a box a little taller than that once one is seen (a first line), remembered per font so a paragraph's sentences match; in Books, which gives no font, the block's first line, measured once per block. Chromium's boxes are on the glyphs already. Native text views and both PDF readers give glyph-height boxes. `fixtures/pages/leading.html` guards it (`# boxes:`).
- **Block breaks come from roles, not hop counts:** static text, links, list markers and `…StyleGroup` subroles (code, strong, emphasis) are inline; anything else is a block. Both engines expose the same structure, except that Chromium shows a `<b>`, `<abbr>` or `<span>` with an id (Wikipedia's) as a plain `AXGroup`: it's inline when it holds only inline content and sits inside an inline element or between inline neighbours (`isInlineGroup`). A `<p>` beside bare text has a block on its other side.
- **Reading scope (#10):** reading stays in the nearest `AXDocumentArticle`, else `AXLandmarkMain`, and skips chrome inside it: nav/banner/footer/complementary landmarks, controls (buttons…), visually hidden text, timestamps, short link rows (several links and no sentence; a list item that is one link is content, #52). A chrome container the user started in is read. Judge a sentence by the leaf one character *inside* its end (the end marker sits on a boundary), climbing past Chromium's per-line `AXStaticText` boxes. On top, a text layer (#11) measures each sentence's block like a Readability paragraph (`UnitText`: words, link density, sentence punctuation, column), memoized: between the start and the first paragraph only headings, list items and sentences are read, and past it a block that isn't a sentence ends the reading if no paragraph (or prose list) follows in scope.
- **What isn't prose is a cue (#24, #26):** code blocks (WebKit `AXPreformattedStyleGroup`; Chromium a group whose only child is `AXCodeStyleGroup`) and table cells aren't read: the walk returns one cue `Sentence` (`cue` set, no words, its range covering the run) before the next readable sentence, and the session plays `Player.cueSound`, a soft double tap about as loud as speech (at a third of that, it went unheard on a laptop's speakers), with the highlight on the block, unstretched, then 0.15 s (`Pacing.cuePause`). A figure caption gets a cue, then is read; so does the first text after a figure, video or picture with nothing to read in it (`media(before:)`: raphaelsalaja.com captions a drawing with a loose `<sub>` after it), unless the reading starts there; while its cue plays, the highlight covers the figure itself (`Sentence.shows`). A caption is one block of text (`holdsOnlyInline`): Anthropic's metadata columns beside the cover image aren't one. A one-line code block of 60 characters or less is read. Pointing at a block cues it too. The narrator's performance carries on across cues. `--extract` prints a cue as "♪ code"; fixture expected files include them, and the corpus scorer leaves them out.
- **WebKit drops plain `<div>`s from the tree,** so a container's inline children (byline links, tag rows) sit beside its paragraphs and their "block" is the whole article: judge the run of inline siblings between two blocks (`TextUnit`). WebKit also puts rendered `aria-hidden` text in the range string ("Copy linkIntroduction") though no leaf holds it, so a heading speaks its leaves' text.
- **Chromium clips element frames to the nearest scrolling container** (T3 Code's chat, not just the viewport): text out of view comes back full width but 0–1 px tall. Never treat "flat" as hidden; visually hidden text is ~1 px *wide*. Text-marker bounds are not clipped. `fixtures/pages/scroller.html` guards this.
- **Pages rebuild their elements under you** (T3 Code re-renders messages while an agent works). Stale markers read as `""`. `WebDocument.relocate` finds the sentence's text in the new tree and rebuilds fresh leaf-anchored markers; `sentence(after:scope:)` uses it to keep walking. `fixtures/pages/rerender.html` guards this.
- **All Accessibility work runs on `AXWorker` (#12), never the main thread.** Documents are confined to that serial queue: create and call them inside `AXWorker.shared.run { }`; the main actor only touches results (sentences, rects). Dev modes in `main.swift` may call documents directly because they're single-threaded. `--simulate` prints a main-thread stall meter.
- **The player gets one sentence at a time (#15):** a buffer on `AVAudioPlayerNode` can't be taken back. The next sentence is scheduled when the current one's speech is *consumed* (its closing pause, never under 120 ms, gives the slack for gapless playback); synthesized audio waits in the session, numbered and raw, so a speed change stretches it differently and a voice change can redo it. `--simulate … --speed-change-at T` exercises this.
- **WebKit exposes embedded objects as U+FFFC ("￼")** (icon buttons, images) and its character step **stalls at them**, one object at a time. Treat "￼" as blank when tokenizing and measuring, step past objects with `step(past:)` (falls back to the next text leaf in document order), and never let `walk` jump to the window's end. `fixtures/pages/icons.html` guards this (modelled on raphaelsalaja.com, where Safari skipped the first three paragraphs).
- **Chromium browsers give no text positions until pulsed** (Aside, Chrome, Arc, Dia, unless VoiceOver is on). `AXManualAccessibility` is Electron-only (browsers answer −25205, unsupported); their tree is built without inline text boxes, so every text-range bounds query returns a zero-size box while element frames are right. `WebDocument.probeTextGeometry` detects this once per page and highlights whole text blocks instead (`elementFrames`: the sentence's `TextUnit`, so a loose `<sub>` in the article is its own box, not the article's). Their frames are clipped to the scroll container: a block scrolled away comes back as a sliver on the edge it left by, and is reported just past that edge, so the highlight hides, following stops, and a reading past the fold scrolls; a block touching an edge is reported as reaching past it. `fixtures/hosts/electron --browser --scroll-after S` reproduces this mode. Only Chromium's screen-reader mode turns inline text boxes on, so the probe pulses the browser into it (next bullet; `WebDocument.TextGeometry`: unknown, real, blocks, awaiting): block highlights until it switches, probed at 2.1 s after the pulse and then every 0.25 s up to 4 s, then word boxes, picked up by the session partway through the sentence (the lines change shape, which drops its cached word boxes). Boxes gone again later (something sent a lone false): pulse again, at most every 10 s per pid. A pulse that never brings positions is logged once and that process stays on blocks. In tests the `--browser` host must give none (`# also: Browser`, `media.html`); `# also: Pulsed` runs it pulsed with `--pulse-electron` (Electron 39 answers the pulse as Chrome does) and must give them (`leading.html`).
- **A "next" marker can lie behind the one you asked from.** From the empty line between two `<br>`s, WebKit's `AXNextTextMarkerForTextMarker` returns the start of the paragraph before them. `AXTextMarkerRangeForUnorderedTextMarkers` hides this, so `step(past:)` rejects a step whose ordered range starts at the new marker (`isAfter`). Same in Chromium tables: the next leaf in tree order after the last cell is the first cell again (`AXColumn` lists the cells). `fixtures/pages/breaks.html` and `flow.html` guard this.
- **Chromium's range string drops the space at a line wrap between two elements** ("sevenevery") though the leaves' `AXValue` keep it, and its "paragraphs" can be single text runs. `chunk` records where a space was lost (the spoken text puts it back) and grows past 12 paragraphs while its first sentence is unfinished.
- **Chromium browsers build a page's tree only when a client walks into the window,** and switch it off when unused. The flag doesn't work on them (−25205), and their window stays an `AXWindow` with the browser's own controls, so the stub check can't see it. After Aside relaunched, every hold hit an empty `AXScrollArea` where the page was. When a lookup finds nothing in a Chromium app (`AX.isChromium`: a `<name> Framework.framework`) and its window has no web area, `AX.wakeWebArea` walks the window and waits up to 1.5 s for one (at most every 2 s per app); the hold then hit-tests again.
- **Enable Electron's tree with `AXManualAccessibility`. `AXEnhancedUserInterface` only as true, true, false (`AX.pulseScreenReader`)**, back to back on `AXWorker`, never anything else. Held at true it makes AppKit animate every window move (the old ban's reason). Chromium counts the requests (+1 true, −1 false), re-arms a 2 s timer on each, and when it fires with the count above 0 sets the count to 0 and turns on complete + screen-reader mode for the whole browser, for good: the count goes 1, 2, 1, the mode is on ~2.05 s later, and AppKit's flag ends at 0, so windows move as before (31 vs 32 ms, unanimated). **Never send a lone false:** at count 0 it switches the mode off at once (a plain true-then-false never works). All three answer −25208 (not implemented) and take effect. Pulse only from a lookup or reading that met a page with no text positions in a Chromium browser (not Electron: older releases take a false as "screen reader stopped"), never on launch or for an app the user isn't reading in; not while VoiceOver is on; not if the flag already reads true (another assistive tool holds it); if it reads 1 afterwards, send true, false. Measured (docs/design.md §9): word boxes within 1 px median of `getClientRects`, the mode held 41 min through reloads, navigations and new windows, ~+3% memory. Screen capture/OCR and a browser extension are ruled out for this.
- **The narrator is given sayable text, not the page's (#18, `Speakable`).** It already reads numbers, units, currencies, percentages, versions, "#48" and "p95" well (`--hear`), so those are left alone. Rewritten: abbreviations ("e.g." came out "E G"), ISO dates ("eleven hundred five"), "82M"/"7B", identifiers (`CORS_ORIGIN`, `getUserName()`, `goLive(_:_:)`, `KEY=1`), file paths and names (`Sources/Unread/App.swift:566` → "Sources slash Unread slash App dot swift, line 566"), flags, code operators, "*", and README/TODO/FIXME/WIP. Brand names stay whole ("GitHub", "OpenAI", "macOS"): humps are split only in lower-case identifiers of three words or more. Paths need a file name or a root (`~/`, `./`, `/`), so "and/or" and "NGINX/OpenResty" stay. Each display word maps to its spoken words, so the aligner times what was said and a display word starts with its first spoken word; the prebuffer and the predicted end of a live sentence count spoken characters. Run `cat fixtures/corpus/*.expected.txt | Unread --speakable` after a rule change: it changes 17 of 897 corpus lines, all intended.
- **Word highlight (#20): the voice's words aren't the page's.** Numbers expand ("$45.99" → 5 words) and symbols are said or dropped, so the aligner's words are matched back to display words by spelling (`WordTiming.align`). A display word's box (`WordBox`) is one per line it's set on: a word hyphenated at a line end ("Eventu-" / "ally" in Books, "para-" / "graph" in a PDF) has two, and the mark moves to the second when the voice is through the first's share of its letters.
- **The mark follows `AVAudioPlayerNode`'s timeline, not a timer.** That timeline keeps running when the node runs dry, reports nil while paused, and restarts at 0 on `stop`; `Player.claim` accounts for all three. Subtract the output's presentation latency (Bluetooth). The mark is a pure function of that position (`WordMark.frame`), drawn every display frame; don't hand its motion to Core Animation's implicit animations. It runs 100 ms ahead of the voice by default ("Mark ahead of voice": 0, 50, 100, 150 ms): a mark that lags is noticed from 45 ms, one that leads from 125 ms (ITU BT.1359). A saved none from before was the old default and was moved to 100 ms once (`highlightLeadMoved`).
- **The highlight hides while the user scrolls the page being read.** Re-measuring lags a scroll by 100–400 ms (at most 10 a second; in Aside up to 200 ms each), so a highlight drawn during one sits on whatever text slides under it. A global scroll-wheel monitor (`ReadingSession.userScrolled`) hides it from the first event over the document. The page keeps moving after the last event (Chromium animates a wheel scroll ~0.3 s) and Aside moves element frames only twice a second, so a measurement isn't trusted until it has held for longer than the app's update step (learned from how often positions move: `updateStep`; 0.55 s until seen). A trackpad reports how far each event moves the page, momentum included, so 0.1 s after the last event the highlight is drawn where it was, moved vertically by that much times the page's scale (a zoomed page moves less: `scrollScale`, learned from each settled prediction; sideways drift is ignored) (`ScrollState.predicted`), then confirmed or corrected by a trusted measurement; a mouse wheel's steps don't say, so it waits (`settling`). Nothing says where a page ends, so a scroll into its top or bottom overshoots once; the correction records the limit (`limits`, carried to each next sentence by how far apart they measured) and later predictions stop there.
- **The reading's highlight belongs to the window being read.** It's drawn in its own panel the size of that window (`Overlay.attach`), at the normal window level, `.transient` (out of Mission Control) and click-through, ordered just above the window with `order(.above, relativeTo:)`, which works across processes only within a level: at `.statusBar` the level wins. So windows above it cover the highlight. The order survives other windows being raised and is lost when the window itself is raised: the panel is ordered again on each ⌃ hold, when any app activates, when the window comes back on screen, and when the 10 Hz order check (`WindowWatch`: is the window in front of the panel?) finds it above; measured in a hidden host, 9–95 ms after the window raised itself. The aiming preview stays in one panel per screen at `.statusBar`, as does a reading whose window isn't known. `DocumentFinder.booksWindow(at:)` takes the topmost layer-0 window at the point, so it skips Unread's own.
- **The highlight moves with the window.** Accessibility says a window moved only once a drag is over, so `WindowWatch` asks the window server (`CGWindowListCopyWindowInfo(.optionIncludingWindow)`, ~0.1 ms) off the main thread: 120 times a second while a mouse button is down or the window moved in the last 0.5 s, else 30. The panel follows each change (`Overlay.follow`), and every rect the session keeps (lines, word boxes, the visible area, predictions, page limits) is relative to the window's top-left as it was when measured, so a move redraws nothing. In a hidden host gliding 240 px over a second, the panel trailed by p50 9–23 ms, p95 18–27 ms (the window server shows a `setFrame` about two frames later; the host's own move took ~15 ms to show), 31–39 ms for the first step from the 30 Hz rate (`scripts/window-check.sh`). SkyLight's move notifications would save at most the ≤8 ms poll interval, so it isn't used. A measurement is drawn only if it's trusted (`ReadingSession.judge`): not when the window moved during it or in the 0.1 s before it arrived (the panel carries the highlight meanwhile); not when the window server and the app's own `AXPosition` disagree on how far the window moved since the last trusted one (the server shows a move a few ms after the app makes it, so the text was measured a step past the frame; two measurements in a row agreeing means the window is at rest, and re-anchors); not when it puts the sentence where it was on screen before the window moved (Books' text boxes lag its view for a refresh or two; trusted again 1 s after the move). A size change reflows the text: the highlight hides and comes back from a measurement started 0.1 s after the last change, in a window of the new size.
- **The highlight's panels live on one Space** (no `.canJoinAllSpaces`): a swipe to another desktop slides the highlight away with the page it's on. Each ⌃ hold moves the screen panels to the current Space (`Overlay.claimActiveSpace`: made afresh when `isOnActiveSpace` is false); the reading's panel is made afresh on the current Space when its window is on screen there and the panel isn't (a window on every Space, a window moved to another desktop). The reading also hides the highlight while the window being read is off screen (minimized, hidden, or on another Space: the window server's on-screen flag for the window from `_AXUIElementGetWindow`, as `WindowWatch` sees it), and while the sentence can't be measured or found again (the page was left).
- **Answers still being written (#17).** A last sentence that doesn't end like one (".", "!", "?", "…", ":") and has nothing after it may still be being written: it's held back until it does, text follows, or it reads the same for 0.7 s (`ReadingSession.growth`). At the end of the text, a page that changed during the reading (a sentence found again after a rebuild, a growing last sentence, new text after the end) is checked every 0.35 s for more, until 6 s after it last changed; a static page finishes at once. `fixtures/pages/streaming.html` (`--simulate --pid` while it streams) exercises it.
- **Chromium's PDF viewer is its own document (#85, `PDFiumDocument`).** It nests three web areas (extension page, viewer toolbar and thumbnails, the PDF); both the window search and a hit resolve to the innermost by the viewer's extension URL (`chrome-extension://mhjfbmdgcfjbbpaeojofohoefgiehjai/`), so a page's iframe is never taken. The viewer with no PDF area in it (still loading, a password prompt, a PDF that failed) is no document: never read its prompt, never wait for it on a lookup, and look again only 2 s later. The PDF is one `AXLandmarkRegion` per page, added ~150–300 ms apart after it opens (their parent's description, "PDF document containing 15 pages", counts them from the start), one group per PDF paragraph; `AXHeading` is PDFium's guess and often wrong. Text keeps line ends as `\r\n` and a line-end hyphen as U+0002 (a plain "-" at a block's end). So sentences are cut from blocks, not the marker stream, and `PDFLayout` decides from text and boxes alone: page numbers, footers, headers repeated on a neighbouring page (or at the top or foot of the page two away: books alternate the book's title and the chapter's), footnotes, figure labels and table rows beside a "Figure N:"/"Table N:" caption (one cue), and author lines before the first paragraph are skipped; "References" ends the reading; a paragraph ending mid-sentence carries on into the next block that starts in lower case, past all of those. **The last lines of a page are often text** (a paragraph's first lines, a heading, a short paragraph): a footer must also sit off the paragraphs' left margin, or be set smaller; a footnote's marker ("§ 3.", "4GB", "2FA" look like one) needs a smaller font, or, with fonts untrusted, a place at its column's foot set off by more space than the column leaves between blocks (a run of "§" sections down to the foot is text). **Font sizes are exact in TeX's PDFs and noisy in Quartz's** (the fixtures' 10-point text reads 9.3 and 10.7, their 8-point footer 10.7), so fonts decide only on a page whose long paragraphs all agree (table rows and blocks starting in lower case don't count). TeX leaves less space above a footnote than between paragraphs, so its footnotes are found by font. Whatever was worked out while a page it needed wasn't built yet isn't kept; a reading waits for such a page only while pages keep coming, never while pointing or in a hold's speculation (`AXWorker.speculating`). A lookup finds the pages on screen by halving over the regions and reads only the page under the pointer (149 pages: 621 → 65 AX calls a lookup). A hyphen joins its word unless the document spells it with one, either half has one, or both halves are words and the joined form isn't; before "and"/"or"/"to" it stays apart ("pre- and post-training"), as in PDFKit, unless the word goes on with a hyphen ("English-to-German"). Text-marker bounds aren't clipped to the scroller; element frames are. A run that starts with "\r\n" is measured ~330 px left of where it's drawn, so boxes that miss their block's frame are moved back. `fixtures/pages/paper.pdf`, `report.pdf`, `slides.pdf` and `book.pdf` guard this, with `--pdf-check`.
- **PDFKit pages (Preview, Safari, WKWebView; #86) answer one text call.** Every page is an `AXPage` under a container (`AXSharedDocumentContainer` in Preview, `AXPDFPluginSubrole` in WebKit), all present from the start, and WebKit's appear only ~0.3 s after a client first walks the window. A page gives its whole text, in reading order, from `AXAttributedStringForRange` with `AXNumberOfCharacters`, a font (name, size) per run and the links; it answers nothing else (−25213), and there are no text markers. The text holds the page's furniture: running header, footer, page number, footnotes, figure labels, author grid. `PDFKitDocument` judges each line by its font: smaller than the body size by 1.5 pt is furniture; bold, or larger by 0.75 pt, is a heading (TeX papers come through as "Helvetica" at every weight, so size is the only cue there); a block with an email, a footnote marker on a smaller line, a page number alone; a running header or footer set like the text (a book's), known by repeating at the top or foot of a page one or two away in the same size, digits aside (books alternate their title and the chapter's; a paper's title is larger than its running header, so it stays). Line ends are spaces except at a link, a column end or a block PDFKit split oddly, where they're `\n`, and two paragraphs can share one `\n`-line (a superscript between them), so lines are joined into a block until one ends like a sentence, and a block that doesn't runs on into the next paragraph across a column or page break. A `\n`-line can hold several things (a caption and the table rows or text around it, a bold run-in heading and its paragraph, a footnote, stacked figure labels): `PDFPage.split` cuts them. Body text that ends no sentence but reads like prose is a paragraph (a sentence a page end cut). A unit skips the captions between its parts and reads them after the sentence crossing them; a sentence's boxes are one per run of page text. Only a hyphen at a real line end (a `\n` in the leaf's text) is resolved, never one before "and"/"or"/"to": the document's own spelling first (its first pages, then each page as it's parsed; neither half of a broken word counts as a word), then a few prefixes ("multi-" + a word the paper uses alone), then `/usr/share/dict/words` with inflections (Webster's 1934: it has "multihead"), else the hyphen stays; French joins only on the paper's spelling (`Hyphens`). Boxes: the `AXStaticText` leaves measure sub-ranges per line (`AXRangeForLine`, `AXBoundsForRange`; `AXLineForIndex` and `AXRangeForPosition` lie), and their text carries `\n` at every line end where the page has a space, so each leaf is matched to the page string ignoring whitespace. Leaves drop text (the run before a paragraph's first link), and a leaf after a dropped run can come with its box in PDF coordinates, off the page: text mostly in no measurable leaf gets its paragraph group's frame. Scrolling: Preview's `AXScrollArea` takes `AXMakeScreenRectVisible`; WebKit's plugin only scrolls whole pages (`AXScrollToVisible` on the `AXPage`, done only when the page's top is below the view or the page is off screen, else it would scroll back up), so a sentence in the hidden foot of the page on view stays where it is; a sentence across a page break scrolls to the page of its part in view, never back to the page above. **Preview gives no frames on a page out of view** (some leaves lose theirs once scrolled away), so a sentence there is scrolled to before it's measured (`measuresOffScreen`, in the session and `--extract`), and whether the user scrolled it away is judged by its page's frame (`place(of:)`), which Preview always gives. Don't let a PDF parse depend on geometry Preview may not have: a figure page's bottom-first caption is put back in order from the text, and `PDFPage.reorder` is done again once the page has frames, for the blocks nothing has used yet. The reference list is looked up at most eight pages back. Hidden hosts can't be hit-tested, so `paragraph(at:)` finds the leaf under the point by frames. `fixtures/pages/paper.pdf`, `book-pdfkit.pdf` (a book's body-size running headers with their page numbers, sentences across page breaks) and `edges.pdf` (a page ending without a newline, a sentence across a page break and one across a table and caption, hyphens inside lines and at line ends, a figure-only page; its expected file asks for `--word-boxes`) guard all of it in both trees (the native host shows a `.pdf` in a `PDFView`, the engine "Preview").
- **A page the user scrolled away from isn't pulled back.** Each refresh judges `following` by the sentence's lines; by its page's frame when the document can't measure text out of view (Preview, `place(of:)`); and between sentences, while the next is still being made, by the one just read (`justRead`), since that's when a scroll otherwise went unseen and the next sentence scrolled back to itself. A measurement queued before ⌃ went down doesn't scroll during aiming. `scrollIntoView` says whether it scrolled: only then is there a 0.8 s grace before `following` is judged again.
- **Books is read through the iOS bridge's text markers (#87, `BooksDocument`).** Its web areas are UIKit elements that answer no text call, so a Books web area (bundle id checked first, which costs no call) without `AXTextMarkerRangeForUIElement` among its parameterized attributes (`BooksDocument.isBridged`) is a book, never a `WebDocument`. Only the visible chapter's area has children (one leaf per visible paragraph piece, and an `AXImage` per picture); the chapter is one flow of columns, mostly off screen. A picture's marker range is "￼" with the picture's box and no width in indices; a cover or plate is an area holding one `AXImage`, read as a cue (#24) on the picture, then turned past. On a leaf, `AXTextMarkerRangeForUIElement` is the whole paragraph, markers step by character and by line, any range has a box and a string (`AXAttributedStringForTextMarkerRange`; `AXStringForTextMarkerRange` fails), and `AXIndexForTextMarker` is the offset in the chapter's text; there are no word, sentence or paragraph steps and no marker from an index or point, and the index calls (`AXBoundsForRange`, `AXLineRangeForIndex`) are dead. A line's string drops the wrap space before it, whose index its start marker reports; at a hyphenation wrap the marker before a line's first letter sits at the end of the line before, so measure from the second character; a column-top line's box takes in the column's padding. Markers survive page turns. Turn pages with `AXScrollRightByPage` / `AXScrollLeftByPage` on a visible leaf (~50 ms, no focus, works hidden), never with key events. A heading's leaf is an `AXHeading`. A block's start marker can sit before a "\n" (a chapter's text starts with one; blank lines): step past it, or the block is empty. At a chapter's end the next page is another web area, so the flow's end is a position. Only a step that answers −25212 (no value) or doesn't advance is the end: −25204 (Books busy) is asked again, a call that still fails is never taken for the end (that would turn with the rest of the spread unread), and `turnPage` checks the end again before turning. **A lookup never turns the page:** one that reaches the end only says which end it is (`pageEnd`), and the session asks for one turn per end (`turnPage`) once the chapter's last sentence has been heard (its speech consumed, plus the output's latency), never while the user aims, and stops asking 12 s after; later lookups take the next chapter's area, searched only in the book view (the ancestor holding every chapter's area and the page numbers), once its pages have held for 0.1 s from one lookup to a later one (~0.35–0.45 s after the turn). A chapter with nothing to read but a picture has an end of its own, turned past in turn. The book's last page answers `AXScrollRightByPage` with success and changes nothing, so a turn that moves nothing for 2 s is the book's end. Pages turn the way the chapter runs: its area is the view's size, moved left past the view by the columns before in a left-to-right book (right in a right-to-left one); on a chapter's first spread, where it overlaps the view, the text's language says. Which lines of a block are on the page pointed at come from line geometry (a line above the one before it starts the next column), not from a two-page split. A book's text doesn't grow (`mayGrow`), so #17's hold on an unfinished last sentence doesn't apply. `swift scripts/reader-probe.swift <pid> calls|turn` reruns the probe. **Pointing:** Books' system hit test over its pages times out (the 0.25 s cap) or lands on the toolbar, so a hold over a Books window (the window server's topmost window at the point, ~0.6 ms) goes straight to `BooksDocument`, which finds the text by its lines' marker boxes; a leaf's own frame can be relative to its area ("CHAPTER 1" at 163,89, drawn at 495,297), so it's never used. A spread can show two files' pages (a chapter's title page beside its first page), each its own web area: pointing searches every area on screen, every block of each leaf, and a reading runs on into the pages beside before it asks for a turn. Never resize a hidden Books window (single-page view): its leaves stop answering until the book is reopened, and it loses its page. Test only with a book opened by `open -g` and Books hidden; the author's Books library is theirs (no new books). Leave Books unhidden for ~5 s after a book opens: hidden sooner, its web view never lays out the saved page, and the tree stays on the chapter's first spread while turns move only the page numbers (close the book and open it again from the library).
- **Native text views scroll by setting `AXVisibleCharacterRange` (#13)**, which leaves the selection, caret and focus alone. Never move the caret to scroll. `AXScrollToVisible` and page-scroll actions are unsupported on NSTextView. Native positions are `NativeRange`s tagged with a text version, so ranges survive edits made while reading.
- **Pauses are Unread's, not the voice's (#56).** Each sentence's own lead-in and tail quiet is trimmed to 50 ms, and `Pacing` puts 0.5 s between sentences, divided by the speed. The same after a paragraph, heading or list item: 1.0 s there, with a fresh voice, lost 20 of 20 blind pairs (#80), so the reader doesn't track block ends.
- **The narrator (#62) runs on the Neural Engine through Core ML, in-process, with the GPU unused** (`--narrate` reads the GPU driver's registry: 0 clients, 0 ms). Its quirks:
  - **Core ML compiles the models per binary path.** The first load from a new path (`.build/release/Unread`, `build/Unread.app`, a copy) takes ~50 s; after that ~1.4 s. Rebuilding in place doesn't recompile.
  - **The model starts most sentences with 0.1–0.6 s of quiet,** generated at ~52 ms a frame like speech. The stream starts 50 ms before the voice (`QwenStreamTrimmer`), and the chunk size stays at its first, short value until then (`speechStarted`), so the quiet costs its generation time and no more.
  - **The vocoder's upsampler runs on the CPU.** On the Neural Engine each call queues behind the talker (85–130 ms instead of 27), so first audio is ~100 ms later and short chunks are expensive.
  - **Chunks grow slowly and stay small** (`Narrator.chunkFrames`, 2, 2, 3, 4 frames, then 4). Synthesis runs at ~0.7× real time, and the chunk with the voice's onset can hold under 100 ms of audio, so a chunk that grows faster than playback leaves the player waiting (`[2, 4, 8, 12]` did in every sentence). Bigger chunks also arrive in bursts (a 12-frame one takes ~650 ms), which a player reading sped up can't ride out; capping at 4 costs no speed.
  - **The talker's memory is a fixed 1024 slots** shared by the prompt (the clip's ~73 frames, both transcripts) and the generated frames (12.5 a second). A sentence that doesn't fit at 1.5 frames a character is said in clause-sized parts (`QwenReadRules.split`, 200 characters) through one `QwenTalkSession`, which carries each part into the next. Unsplit, the audio would stop mid-sentence (`contextFull`).
  - Seeds are per talk session (`QwenTalkSession.stableSeed` of its first sentence), so a reading started from the same sentence always sounds the same, quiet start included.
  - **A reading is one performance (#56, #80).** Each sentence is still its own take and stream, but `Narrator.speak(_:after:)` renders it in the talk session the previous sentence used when that was the sentence just said, in full, in the same language and voice: the talker hears the previous sentence's text and codec frames first, as between a long sentence's parts. That holds across paragraphs, headings and list items. A jump, a cancel or a voice change starts a fresh session. Costs nothing measurable (`--simulate`: same real-time factor, first chunks no later); carried sentences come out ~10% shorter.
- **Reading with the narrator (#62): only the sentence playing streams.** A narrator take (`NarratorTake`) is queued as soon as it's asked for and fills in as it streams. If it's still incomplete when its turn comes (a reading's first sentence, and the next one or two, before synthesis gets ahead), it goes *live* (`ReadingSession.live`) once `Narrator.prebuffer` has arrived: each chunk is handed to the player as it comes, and nothing else is scheduled until the last one is. Takes waiting behind it stay off the player, raw, so #15 holds. A chunk that lands after the player ran dry starts later on its timeline; `Live.runs` records where each run landed so the word starts follow. A long first sentence isn't split to start sooner: the narrator's first audible chunk takes as long for 31 characters as for 194.
- **The narrator says one sentence at a time, so a hold must get it first.** When a hold starts a narrator speculation, the reading yields (`yieldNarrator`): it stops the sentence it's synthesizing ahead and starts no new one until aiming ends, so the speculation isn't queued behind it (`--jump-at`: first audio after the jump 218 ms instead of a cold ~600). A sentence still streaming into the player can't be stopped without cutting it, so a jump early in a reading gets no head start. A dropped speculation stops its own stream (`Speculation.deinit`), and a release that jumps stops the old reading before aiming ends, so it can't refill the narrator first.
- **An idle narrator goes cold two ways, so ⌃ going down wakes it (`Narrator.wake`).** After ~10 s idle the Neural Engine runs its calls ~2× slower (prefill 125 → 250–900 ms). And its mapped files (Core ML's compiled vocoder programs, 422 MB, and the embedding tables) are file-backed: on a Mac short of memory, macOS drops them within a minute, and the next sentence reads them back a page at a time (~17k page-ins, +0.5–1 s). On key-down the narrator asks the kernel to read back the pages sentences have used (`WorkingSet`, `madvise(WILLNEED)`; ~600 MB, not all 1.17 GB mapped: the 311 MB text table is ~1% used) and says a 4-frame line nobody hears, which reaches both vocoder variants (w12, w20). The speculation starts ~0.3 s later. The warm-up alone is worse than nothing when the files are out: it faults them in one page at a time in front of the speculation. `--narrate --idle … --evict` measures it.
- **Speed starts at 1.2×** (`speed` in the defaults; `speed.narrator` from before carries over): at 1× the London storyteller speaks 16.6 voiced c/s and Kokoro spoke 19.7 (`--pace`). It's tuned to the storyteller, the slowest preset (presets' `pace` 15.8–20.2).
- **The narrator has no speed control, so its speed is a time stretch** (`TimeStretch`, WSOLA, on the raw audio as it's played). Speed changes don't redo any audio. The output depends only on the input, not on how it was chunked. `AVAudioUnitTimePitch` was rejected: offline, it glitched at chunk joins unless kept ~8k samples behind the input (~0.3 s more first audio); in the graph, it would stretch `Pacing`'s pauses again and move the word mark's clock off the player's timeline.
- **The narrator's words are timed by forced alignment (#58),** since Qwen3 reports no durations. `Aligner` runs FluidAudio's Parakeet CTC 110M (English only: no CTC model covers French) on a take's audio and finds the Viterbi path through the sentence's tokens (`CTCAlignment`), after NeMo normalization ("$5" → "five dollars"); `WordTiming.align` maps the spoken words back to display words. A take is `streamed` when its audio is in (the narrator moves on) and `isComplete` once aligned (~70 ms later). A live take is aligned again every 0.75 s of new audio (`complete: false`: the word the audio stops in is left out), and words past the alignment are spread by characters from the last aligned one. Against Kokoro's exact starts (before it was dropped): median 50 ms off, p95 145 ms (80 ms CTC frames); a reading's scheduled starts against its recording: median 44 ms, p95 129 ms (estimate: 166 / 400 ms). The encoder shares the Neural Engine with the narrator without slowing it (RTF 0.67–0.76 vs 0.66–0.74). French and a failed load keep `WordTiming.estimated`.
- **Voice presets (#60) are data:** `Resources/Voices/<id>.json` (name, language, summary, description, transcript, preview, pace), the clip the Base model clones (`<id>.wav`, mono 24 kHz PCM16: the clip encoder refuses other formats) and a preview (`<id>-preview.m4a`) the narrator recorded, so Settings plays it with no synthesis. **The transcript must be exactly what the clip says,** or the clone garbles: `make-voices.sh` keeps only takes Whisper hears word for word. **A clone keeps its clip's tempo,** so descriptions ask for a natural, steady pace and takes outside 13–19 voiced c/s are dropped. VoiceDesign often makes a "man" at 180–270 Hz; takes on the wrong side of 175/150 Hz are dropped.
- **The narrator reads each language in that language's preset** (`NarratorVoices`; French falls back to the English voice if it has none). `Narrator.use` switches between sentences on the narrator's own queue, so the sentence in flight finishes in the old voice; it costs ~330 ms of warming (prompt rows, talker memory prefix, vocoder priming), and the encoded clip is cached on disk after its first ~1 s. Then the session redoes that language's queued narrator takes (`narratorVoiceChanged`). Each take carries its voice's `pace` for the prebuffer and the word estimate.
