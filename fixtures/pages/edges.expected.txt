Reading a Paper at the Edges of Its Layout
Abstract
A reader of papers meets the layout where it is least regular, from the head of a column to its foot.
This one checks the joins a typesetter leaves behind and the blocks that stand in the way of a sentence.
1 Hyphens
Some checks run both pre- and post-training, and some on first- and second-order terms alike.
A multi-head layout keeps its hyphen, while a sentence broken at its end is joined again.
The checks we ran pre- and post-training agree.
2 Tables
Tables are set apart from the text around them.
When a table stands in the middle of a sentence, the reading finishes the sentence first and then announces the table.

Table 1: Words and lines read whole, per viewer.
A paragraph that starts at the foot of a page and goes on at the top of the next one is read as one sentence, without the page number.
3 Figures
A page that holds only a figure is announced, and its caption is read whole.
