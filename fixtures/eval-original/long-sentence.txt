When the storm finally reached the coast, after three days of warnings that most of the town had chosen to ignore, the wind tore the roof off the old cannery, flooded the lower streets as far as the church, carried a fishing boat clean across the harbour wall and left it resting, upright and almost undamaged, in the car park of the supermarket, where it stayed for most of the winter because nobody could agree on whose job it was to move it, and where the children of the town, most of whom had never once been allowed to set foot on a real boat, climbed all over it every afternoon until the spring.
